The best AI tools for students in 2026 are ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude as an everyday assistant, Perplexity for sourced research, NotebookLM for studying from your own notes, Grammarly for clearer writing, Otter.ai for lecture transcripts and GitHub Copilot for coding. Most have a free plan good enough for coursework, and several have student discounts.

You do not need all of them. This guide groups the tools by the job you actually need done, shows what is free, and ends with simple rules for using AI without getting into trouble at school.

## The quick answer: best AI tool for each job

- **Everyday questions and explanations:** ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude (all have free plans with daily limits).
- **Research with sources you can check:** Perplexity.
- **Studying from your own notes and PDFs:** NotebookLM.
- **Writing, grammar and tone:** Grammarly.
- **Recording and summarising lectures:** Otter.ai.
- **Organising notes and projects:** Notion.
- **Coding help:** GitHub Copilot (free for eligible students).
- **Slides and posters:** Canva.

If you can only pick one, start with a general assistant and add a second tool only when you hit a clear gap.

## 1. General AI assistants: ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude

A general assistant is the tool you will open most often. Use it to explain a hard concept in simpler words, make practice questions, outline an essay, check your working on a maths problem or turn messy notes into a clean summary.

- **ChatGPT** is the most familiar all-rounder. It is strong for explanations, practice tests and brainstorming. The free plan has daily usage caps, and Plus costs about $20 per month.
- **Gemini** works best if your life is already in Google Docs, Drive and Gmail. It also handles long documents well.
- **Claude** is popular for reading long PDFs and dense articles, and for careful writing feedback. It has a free plan with daily limits. Claude does not currently have a public individual student price, so free access mostly comes through partner universities.
- **Muse AI** is the newer option from Meta. If you want to see how it stacks up, read our [Muse AI vs ChatGPT vs Gemini comparison](/muse-ai-vs-chatgpt-vs-gemini).

**Student tip:** ask the AI to teach you, not to do the task for you. A prompt like "Explain this step by step, then give me three practice questions" builds skill. A prompt like "Write my answer" builds a habit that will hurt you in exams.

## 2. Perplexity: research with sources

Perplexity is an AI search tool. Instead of a long chat answer, it gives you a short answer with numbered sources, which makes it a good starting point for a literature search or a quick fact check.

It has a free plan with limited advanced searches. Its Pro plan is normally about $20 per month, and verified students have been offered an Education plan at about $10 per month through SheerID verification. Check Perplexity's own page for the current price in your country.

**Student tip:** never cite Perplexity itself. Open the sources it lists, read them, and cite the originals.

## 3. NotebookLM: study from your own material

NotebookLM is one of the most useful tools for students because it answers only from the files you give it. Upload lecture slides, readings or your own notes, then ask questions, request a summary or turn the material into a study guide, quiz or audio overview.

Google renamed the product Gemini Notebook in July 2026, so you may see either name. The free plan has been reported at up to 100 notebooks with up to 50 sources each, with higher limits on paid Google AI plans. Limits change, so check the app.

**Why it helps:** because answers are grounded in your own sources, it is less likely to invent facts than a general chatbot. You should still check important points against the original page.

## 4. Grammarly: clearer writing

Grammarly checks spelling, grammar, clarity and tone as you type, in your browser, in Word and on your phone. The free plan covers the core checks. Rewrites and plagiarism checking sit behind the paid plan, and student pricing appears only now and then through partners, so look for an institutional licence before you pay.

**Student tip:** use it to fix your own writing, not to generate it. If your school uses AI detection, text that was heavily rewritten or generated by AI can be flagged, and detectors are not always accurate. Keep your drafts and notes as proof of your own work.

## 5. Otter.ai: lecture recording and summaries

Otter records and transcribes lectures and meetings, then gives you a searchable transcript and a summary. The free plan has been reported at about 300 minutes per month with a 30-minute cap per conversation, which suits a few lectures a week. Pro plans add more minutes, and a student discount of about 20 percent has been offered with a school email.

**Student tip:** ask your teacher before you record. Some schools and lecturers do not allow it.

## 6. Notion: notes, tasks and projects

Notion is not mainly an AI tool, but it is where many students keep notes, assignment trackers and group projects. Individual students can use the Plus features free with a school email. The built-in AI is a separate paid add-on, so do not assume it comes with the free education plan. If you only want AI summaries of your notes, NotebookLM or a general assistant may cover it for less.

## 7. GitHub Copilot: coding help

For computer science students, GitHub Copilot suggests code, explains errors and writes tests inside your editor. Eligible students can get Copilot Pro free through the GitHub Student Developer Pack, which also bundles other developer tools. You need to verify your student status with GitHub.

**Student tip:** read and understand every suggestion before you accept it. If you cannot explain the code, you will struggle in the exam and the interview.

## 8. Canva: presentations and visuals

Canva makes slides, posters and infographics from templates, with AI tools to help with layout and images. Canva has said it is free for K-12 students and teachers through school verification, and anyone can use the free plan. Figma also offers a free education plan for students and educators if you do interface design.

## How to use AI without getting into trouble

1. **Read your school's AI policy.** Rules differ between schools, courses and even assignments. When in doubt, ask your teacher.
2. **Disclose when required.** If you used AI for brainstorming, editing or code, say so in the way your course asks.
3. **Verify facts.** AI can sound confident and still be wrong, and it can invent quotes and references. Check names, dates, numbers and citations against a real source.
4. **Keep your own thinking in the work.** Use AI to explain, quiz you and give feedback. Do the analysis and the final writing yourself.
5. **Protect your data.** Do not paste passwords, ID numbers or private documents into any chatbot. Check the privacy settings and switch off data sharing where you can.

## How to choose on a budget

- **$0:** one general assistant free plan, NotebookLM, Grammarly free, Otter free and GitHub Copilot if you qualify.

Start free for a month, notice where the limits actually slow you down, and only then pay for the one tool that fixes that problem.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best free AI tool for students?

For most students, a general assistant such as ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude on its free plan is the best starting point, with NotebookLM added for studying from your own notes. Free plans have daily limits, but they cover most coursework.

### Is ChatGPT free for students?

ChatGPT has a free plan that anyone can use, with daily usage caps. A promotion for free months of ChatGPT Plus was offered to eligible US college students, but its claim deadline of October 31, 2026 has passed.

### Is using AI for homework cheating?

It depends on your school's rules and how you use it. Using AI to explain a topic, quiz you or give feedback is usually accepted. Submitting AI-written work as your own usually is not. Check your course policy and disclose AI use when asked.

### What is the best AI for studying PDFs and lecture notes?

NotebookLM (now also called Gemini Notebook) is built for this. You upload your own material and it answers from those sources. Claude is also well regarded for reading long documents.

### What is the best AI tool for writing essays?

Use AI to plan, get feedback and fix grammar, not to write the essay for you. Grammarly helps with clarity and grammar, and a general assistant can critique your outline and arguments. Always write and check the final version yourself.

### Do students get discounts on AI tools?

Yes. Google, Perplexity, GitHub, Notion, Figma and others have offered student plans or free access, but eligibility, countries and deadlines change often. Verify the current terms on each company's website.

### Which AI tools are free for college students in 2026?

NotebookLM, the free plans of ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude, Grammarly's core checks, Otter's free minutes, Notion's education plan and GitHub Copilot for eligible students are all free or have free access. Google's free student year depends on your country.
